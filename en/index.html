The problem

After go-live is when Ariba shows whether it was well delivered

In the months that follow come the ERP integrations failing with no owner, the suppliers not transacting on SAP Business Network and the approvals stuck with nobody sure why. RB Tech's AMS exists to take operations out of that reactive mode.

AMS method

From go-live to operations under control

Specialist support for SAP Ariba landscapes integrated with the ERP via CIG, in four stages.

01

Diagnosis

  • Review of the landscape and integrations
  • Backlog and recurring tickets mapped
  • Priorities agreed with the business
02

Stabilization

  • Critical backlog handled first
  • Root cause, not just workarounds
  • CIG integration monitoring, with error handling and reprocessing
03

Operations

  • Triage: incident, adjustment or design gap
  • SLAs and a regular follow-up routine
  • Ongoing supplier enablement
04

Evolution

  • Controlled improvements to the landscape
  • Assessment and selective adoption of Ariba quarterly releases
  • Roadmap built with procurement and IT
  1. Reactive
  2. Stable
  3. Predictable
  4. Evolving

The goal is to move out of reactive mode without reopening the project from scratch.

Multinational group

Full suite rollout and AMS across 15+ countries · Americas and Europe · 2022 to 2023

A program that brought Sourcing, Contracts, SLP, Buying with Guided Buying, Invoicing, Catalogs and SAP Business Network to operations on two continents. Rafael Barbieri, founder of RB Tech, was the lead Ariba consultant. The rollout did not happen in a frozen environment: while new waves went live, the countries already in production needed AMS, each with its own tax rules and supplier base.

  • Suite configuration tailored to each country's requirements
  • S/4HANA integration via CIG
  • Supplier enablement on SAP Business Network at every go-live wave
  • Rollout and AMS in parallel, with production countries kept stable
  • Withholding tax data added to the SLP internal supplier questionnaire
